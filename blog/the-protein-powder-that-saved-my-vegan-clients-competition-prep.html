The Protein Powder That Saved My Vegan Client's Competition Prep

By Derek Armstrong • June 02, 2026 • 4 min read
Four weeks out from his first natural bodybuilding show, my client Miguel was panicking. He was vegan. Plant-based for seven years, ethical reasons, not health. He'd been crushing his training. His conditioning was on point. But his protein intake was falling off a cliff. He was eating around 80 grams a day. At his weight, 160 pounds, that's 0.5 grams per pound. Half of what he needed. The Protein Intake Calculator showed him the gap in plain numbers.

 Miguel was losing muscle. He could see it in the mirror. His arms looked smaller. His strength was dropping. His energy was tanking. His coach wanted him to eat eggs and chicken. Miguel wouldn't. His ethics were non-negotiable. The Vegan Protein Adequacy Calculator became his lifeline.

 Here's the problem with plant protein for bodybuilding. It's not that plants don't have protein. They do. Lentils have 18 grams per cup. Tofu has 20 grams per half block. Seitan has 25 grams per serving. The problem is amino acid profile. Specifically, leucine. And lysine. And methionine. Listen, I'm not anti-plant. I'm pro-data. And the data says that plant proteins have lower leucine content per gram than animal proteins. Whey has about 11% leucine. Soy has about 8%. Pea has about 7%. Rice has about 5%. That means to hit the 2-3 gram leucine threshold per meal, you need more total protein from plants. For Miguel, that meant 40-50 grams of plant protein per meal instead of 30-40 from animal sources. He couldn't physically eat that much whole food. He was already full from beans and rice and tofu. He needed a tool. He needed protein powder. The Macro Split Planner helped him rebuild his meal plan around his new target.

 We found a pea-rice protein blend. Pea protein is high in lysine but low in methionine. Rice protein is the opposite. Together, they complement each other. The blend had an amino acid profile almost as good as whey. And it hit 25 grams of protein per scoop. Miguel added two scoops a day. One in the morning with his oatmeal. One after training. That added 50 grams of high-quality plant protein. His total went from 80 to 130. Still not ideal, but better. The PDCAAS scores tell the story. Whey is 1.0. Soy is 0.91. Pea is 0.89. Rice is 0.47. The blend of pea and rice is about 0.95. That's almost as good as animal protein. Within two weeks, Miguel's energy came back. His strength stabilized. His muscles looked fuller. He wasn't losing tissue anymore. He was holding. The Protein Source Comparator helped him choose the right blend.

 The week before the competition, Miguel was stage-ready. Vascular. Conditioned. Full. He placed third in his division. He didn't win, but he held his own against omnivores. The judges didn't know he was vegan until he told them after. He sent me a photo of himself on stage. Lean, confident, smiling. He wrote: “This one's for the plants.” I'm telling you this story because vegans can build muscle. They can compete. They can thrive. But they need to be strategic. You can't just eat salad and hope for the best. You need to know your leucine. You need to know your lysine. You need to know your methionine. The Vegan Protein Adequacy Calculator is free. Use it before you blame your genetics. Miguel still trains. He's planning his next show. He still uses the pea-rice blend. He still doesn't eat animals. And he's still gaining. If you're vegan and struggling to grow, here's my advice. First, get your total protein up. 1.6 grams per kilogram minimum. 1.8 is better. Use the calculator to find your number. Second, focus on leucine. 2-3 grams per meal. That might mean eating more total protein from plants. Third, consider a complementary protein powder. Pea-rice. Soy alone is fine, but blends are better. Fourth, don't forget B12, iron, and zinc. Get blood work. Vegans are often deficient. Fixing those deficiencies will help your energy and recovery. Listen, I eat meat. I'm not vegan. But I've coached vegans to success. It takes more planning. It takes more food. It might take supplements. But it's possible. Miguel proved that.
