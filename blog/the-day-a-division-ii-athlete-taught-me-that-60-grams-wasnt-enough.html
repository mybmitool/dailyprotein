The Day a Division II Athlete Taught Me That 60 Grams Wasn't Enough

By Derek Armstrong • June 02, 2026 • 5 min read
The argument started in the weight room. A Division II offensive lineman, 6'3", 280 pounds, told me he was eating 60 grams of protein a day. Sixty. I thought he was joking. He wasn't. “My nutritionist told me 0.8 grams per kilogram,” he said. “I weigh 127 kilos. That's 102 grams. I'm already under.” He was right. 0.8 grams per kilogram is the RDA for sedentary adults. For athletes, the ISSN recommends 1.6-2.2 grams per kilogram. For a 127-kilogram lineman, that's 203 to 280 grams per day. He was eating less than a third of what he needed.  I pulled out my phone and showed him the math. “You're eating 60 grams. You need at least 200. That's a deficit of 140 grams. You're leaving gains on the table every single day.” He stared at the screen. His jaw tightened. “I've been doing this for two years,” he said. “I thought I was being healthy.” Listen, this happens all the time. Athletes, especially college athletes, are given terrible nutrition advice. They're told to eat “clean” or “balanced” or “the food pyramid.” No one gives them specific protein targets. No one tells them that 0.8 grams per kilogram is for couch potatoes, not for people who squat 500 pounds. The Macro Split Planner can help athletes build a meal plan around their protein target.

 I asked him to write down everything he ate for three days. Here's what he sent me: Day one: oatmeal for breakfast (5g protein), turkey sandwich for lunch (20g), chicken breast and rice for dinner (35g). Total: 60g. Day two: skipped breakfast, protein bar for lunch (20g), pasta with meat sauce for dinner (30g). Total: 50g. Day three: eggs for breakfast (12g), leftover pasta (25g), chicken and vegetables (30g). Total: 67g. Average: 59 grams. He was consistent. Consistently underfed. I built him a sample day at 220 grams. Four meals, 50-60 grams each. Breakfast: 5 eggs (30g) + Greek yogurt (15g) + oatmeal (5g) = 50g Lunch: 8oz chicken breast (50g) + rice + vegetables = 50g Post-workout shake: 2 scoops whey (50g) = 50g Dinner: 8oz lean beef (50g) + potatoes + broccoli = 50g Pre-sleep: cottage cheese (20g) optional “That's a lot of food,” he said. “That's what 280 pounds eats,” I said.  Six weeks later, he texted me. “Bench press went from 315 to 330. Deadlift from 455 to 485. I'm less sore after practice. I have more energy in the fourth quarter. Why didn't anyone tell me this before?” Listen, this is the part that makes me angry. Not at the athlete. At the system. At the “nutritionists” who don't know the difference between RDA and athletic requirements. At the coaches who talk about “discipline” but never mention protein grams. The research has been clear for decades. A 2023 position stand from the International Society of Sports Nutrition states that athletes need 1.6-2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. For strength athletes, the upper end is better. For athletes in a caloric deficit, even higher – up to 2.4 grams per kilogram. That's not a rounding error. That's double what most college athletes eat. I see this in my consulting practice all the time. A client comes in, frustrated, stalled. They're training hard. They're sleeping okay. They're eating “healthy.” But they're eating 0.8 grams per kilogram. They think that's enough because the nutrition label says “20g protein per serving” and they had two servings. Two servings is 40 grams. That's one meal. You need four or five of those. The Protein Intake Calculator is free. Use it before you blame your genetics. My offensive lineman friend? He's now a senior. He's up to 295 pounds, still lean. He's getting looks from NFL scouts. He told me “that argument in the weight room changed my career.” I don't think he's exaggerating. Here's what I want you to take away. If you're training hard and not seeing results, check your protein. Not just whether you're eating it. Whether you're eating enough. 1.6 grams per kilogram is the floor. 2.2 grams per kilogram is the ceiling. For most athletes, 1.8-2.0 is the sweet spot. And don't trust the RDA. The RDA is for sedentary adults. It's not for you. Listen, I'm not saying you need to eat like a bodybuilder. But if you're squatting twice your body weight, you need more protein than someone who isn't. That's not bro science. That's biochemistry. The Macro Split Planner can help you set your protein target first, then fill in the rest. Try it. Track your protein for three days without changing anything. See where you land. I bet you're under by at least 50 grams. My lineman was under by 140 grams. He fixed it. He grew. You can too.
